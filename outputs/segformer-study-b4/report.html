The real-only baseline and SynTIDE model each start from an ImageNet-pretrained MiT-B4 encoder and a new seven-class segmentation head. The trained real-only baseline scores pixels for the FreeMask-style filter. For each class, synthetic target pixels are ignored when teacher cross-entropy exceeds 1.5 times its mean on real training pixels of that class, following the released FreeMask code. The FreeMask paper describes calibration on synthetic pixels. TIDE specifies a tolerance of 1.5 and leaves the calibration source open. The filter excluded 27.08% of synthetic pixels. The synthetic-pretraining checkpoint uses generated targets with an indirect dependency on human labels through its teacher. Fine-tuning initializes from the complete synthetic checkpoint, including its segmentation head. FreeMask paper · FreeMask reference implementation.

Of 54,510 generated images, 54,485 retained at least one pixel that could contribute to the training loss. Every one of the 20,442 training batches contained supervised pixels. Among the foreground categories, aquatic plants had the lowest retained fraction at 4.73%.

The real training split contains all 3,937 official UIIS images and uses the semantic masks distributed in the TIDE generator-training archive. Their sea-floor ID 7 maps to background 0. After that conversion the masks agree with 99.55% of pixels considered valid under the earlier labeling policy. The synthetic pool contains all 54,510 released pairs, ten variations per caption. Primary evaluation uses the same 691 original UIIS validation image identifiers and derived reference masks as the earlier B0 study. Single-scale input is aspect-fitted onto a square padded canvas. The evaluator removes padding, upsamples logits to each original image size, and takes the class with the highest logit per pixel. All 2,073 exported prediction PNGs were independently rescored.

Each synthetic pass used 6,813 batches of eight and a final batch of six. Real-data batches contained eight images. All stages used AdamW with weight decay 0.01, a 100-update warmup and linear decay from peak learning rates of 0.00006 for the encoder and 0.0006 for the decoder. Horizontal flips and mild brightness changes augmented the inputs. The real-only and fine-tuned runs matched their UIIS update budget. Each stage used its final checkpoint, independently of test mIoU.

During synthetic pretraining, mixed-precision arithmetic overflowed. Training resumed from the verified checkpoint at update 7,500, restoring model, optimizer, gradient scaling and random state. SynTIDE pretraining switched to FP16 encoder and FP32 decoder at update 7,674. SynTIDE → UIIS switched to FP16 encoder and FP32 decoder at update 373. The gradient scaler also retried 1 overflowed backward pass. The recovery reused the same augmented batch and preserved the planned sample order, coverage and learning-rate schedule. Final training precision by stage was Real only, mixed FP16 · SynTIDE pretraining, FP16 encoder and FP32 decoder · SynTIDE → UIIS, FP16 encoder and FP32 decoder. Evaluation used FP32 for all three stages. Recovery record · Synthetic precision events.

Stage differences pair predictions on the same visual groups in 2,000 bootstrap draws. They describe variability across sampled test groups for the trained checkpoints. Training-seed uncertainty requires additional B4 runs. The released Table 3 materials establish the backbone, split, label categories, synthetic pretraining direction and dataset scale. Their downstream update budget and exact author-converted semantic test masks remain unavailable.
